02 / The academic arc

From forest to farm to policy.

One line of inquiry runs through the program. Students move from elephants as symbols of Thai kingship, through the 1989 logging ban that displaced more than 2,000 working elephants, to the institutions managing wild populations and human-elephant conflict today.

Temple elephants

Symbols of Buddhist kingship.

Working elephants

The teak era and the 1989 logging ban.

Conservation elephants

Wild populations, welfare, and conflict.

Field methods students learn

  • Bird point counts and mammal line transects
  • Camera-trap deployment and placement
  • Behavioral sampling, ethograms, and individual ID
  • Semi-structured interviews on human-elephant conflict
  • Participatory, community-based monitoring
  • Agroforestry field methods

What students produce

  • An independent research project, designed and run in the field
  • Individual oral presentations across the route
  • Field notebooks kept at every site
  • Two midterm exams and a final

Students weigh the evidence on alternative-livelihood projects, including where they fail and why.

07 / Institutional FAQ

What committees ask first.

Who does our institution contract with?

Ground Truth Advisory, the Bangkok-based practice founded by the program director, is the delivery entity. Contracting, insurance, and liability documentation are provided at the proposal stage.

When can the program run?

The 43-day template adapts to your academic calendar. Feasible windows depend on field conditions along the route and are confirmed in the partner kit and proposal.

What does it cost?

Per-student pricing is driven by cohort size, duration, season, and accommodation mix. In-country transport, accommodation, meals, site access, teaching, and translation are included; international flights, visas, and personal insurance are not. Numbers arrive in the proposal after a fit call.

Do students ride or bathe elephants?

No. Fieldwork is observation-based: Kindred Spirit is an observation-only forest sanctuary, and visits to state captive facilities examine management models rather than staged contact.

How is student research ethics handled?

Interview-based student projects run under your institution’s IRB or ethics pathway where required. The program provides protocol templates, Thai-language consent support, and existing community relationships.

What experience do students need?

None in the field. Methods are taught on the route, and assessment is built into the itinerary.

How do credit and contact hours work?

Duration, credit load, and calendar adapt to your framework; a contact-hour mapping is included in the partner kit.

Who runs it day to day, and what if the lead instructor is unavailable?

Tyler leads the curriculum, Kamolpawn (Mook) Timtang runs in-country operations as a licensed Thai guide, and Mitria Community Connections handles logistics at every site. Contingency staffing is documented in the risk-management plan.
